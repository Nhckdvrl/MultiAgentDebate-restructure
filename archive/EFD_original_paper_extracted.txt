                     Evidence before Opinions: Evidence-First Deliberation for
                       Multi-Agent Decisions under Distributed Information

                                                       Anonymous submission




                             Abstract
  Multi-agent language-model systems often expose local an-
  swers before pooling complementary private evidence. We
  identify a first-message bottleneck: a representation orga-
  nized around a local decision can discard facts that are in-
  conclusive alone but decisive for the team. We introduce
  Evidence-First Deliberation (EFD), a minimal protocol that
  shares decision-free evidence memos before collecting inde-
  pendent ballots, and compare it with an opinion-first baseline
  matched on observations, models, generation seeds, call bud-
  get, and aggregation. On distributed HotpotQA and MuSiQue,
  EFD improves exact match by 12.7 points across six models,
  with an additional one-seed 72B check. On HiddenBench,
  it outperforms this baseline in all 18 model–seed compar-
  isons; the four-Qwen macro-average gain is 36.7 points. A
  separate Mistral-Small-24B transfer gains 10.5 and 8.6 exact-
  match points on HotpotQA and MuSiQue (one seed) and 57.4
  points on HiddenBench (three seeds). A strictly scored non-
  QA diagnostic reproduces the effect and permits direct in-
  terventions on the amount of pooled evidence. Fixed-report,
  private-precommitment, and same-extraction controls show
  two contributors: answer-centered reports omit evidence, and
  public answers interfere with later decisions even when evi-
  dence instructions are matched and measured support is no
  lower. EFD does not help when local answers are effective
  summaries or when the pooled solver fails with complete in-
  formation. These results identify decision visibility relative
  to evidence pooling as a consequential protocol choice under
  distributed information.
